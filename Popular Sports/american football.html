American Football

Overview

Two teams try to score as many points as possible before time passes. Games are split up into two halves, each having two 15-minute time periods, known as "quarters." 22 players will be on the field at any given time, 11 on offense, and 11 on defense. The offense gets 4 plays, known as "downs," to move the ball down the field. This counter is reset with every 10 yards the offense gains, but if the offense runs 4 plays and doesn't gain 10 yards, then the other team gets the ball.

Common Terms

  • Touchdown:

    A player crosses the endzone with the ball, scoring their team 6 points. The scoring team then gets the option to attempt to go for 1 or 2 points.

  • Point After Touchdown (PAT):

    Following a touchdown, the scoring team can kick the ball from the 15 yard line to gain one extra point

  • Two Point Conversion:

    Also known as “going for 2,” teams can choose to run one play from the 2 yard line to gain two extra points after a touchdown

  • Field Goal:

    A team kicks the ball into the uprights (the big yellow bars at the end of the field), gaining 3 points.

  • Line of Scrimmage:

    The line where plays start, moving to where the last play ended.

  • Red Zone:

    The area of the field within 20 yards of the endzone on both sides.

  • Trenches:

    This term refers to the space between the offensive line and defensive line, often closely associated with running the football. If a team is heavily succeeding in this regard, then they are “dominating the trenches."

  • Blitz:

    A blitz is when the defense sends more than 4 defenders to rush towards the backfield, aiming to create pressure for the quarterback or to stop the running back before they cross the line of scrimmage.
